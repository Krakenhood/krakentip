Krakentip · from a blank page

Four tooltips, one family

Tooltip styles for WoW Classic and Forever, designed from scratch. Each one carries everything the game's own tooltip tells you; they differ in how much room they take. They share one look and one set of rules, so you can switch between them freely, and the game can switch for you when you fight.

Concept mockups: nothing here is built yet. You are Krakenhood, a level 21 warrior. Fonts and art are stand-ins for the game's.

What a tooltip is for

When you hover something you are deciding a handful of things in under a second. Every mark on these tooltips answers one of them, and nothing is there that doesn't.

Is it dangerous?The level's colour (grey to red, a skull for far above you) and the rank mark on the frame.
Is it mine to kill?Grey name, grey edge and "tapped" when someone else got the first hit.
Do I need it?The quest ring: your count at a glance, and your group's.
Who is it after?The target under the bar, and a red glow round the frame when it's you.
What can it do for me?For NPCs, their role and services instead of health they'll never lose.

How they work together

The rules they share

The game's essentials, in every style

Whatever the style, you get what the game's own tooltip gives: name, level, type (race and class for players), elite, rare or boss, health, quest progress and who it's attacking. Players add guild, faction and PvP; NPCs their title. The small styles write it shorter, never leave it out.

The edge says who it is to you

A coloured edge on the left, readable from the corner of your eye: hostile, neutral, friendly, tapped. Players get their class colour. When it's attacking you, the whole frame glows red.

The frame says what kind

As on the target frame: a gold dragon corner for elites, silver for rares, a red frame and a skull for bosses. On the small styles a "+" after the level means elite.

Everything has its place

Level top left, quest top right, health in the middle, target below it. When something is missing its space closes, but nothing ever swaps places, so your eyes learn where to look.

Friends get different questions

An innkeeper won't lose health, so NPCs show their role and services instead of a bar. Players show class, guild, PvP flag, and where they are when they're in your party.

The whole party in one row

Group quest progress is a row of small bars, one per member with their initial in class colour, instead of a line per person. A full party still fits in Glance.

What it takes in the game

Everything shown is something the game hands to addons: name, level, rank, health (as a bar when the game hides the numbers), target, tapped, the quest and party lines and the zone line of the game's own tooltip.

Banner attaches to the creature's nameplate, so it needs nameplates switched on. Where a unit has no nameplate (a unit frame, a party member far away, places where the game keeps nameplates from addons), it falls back to Glance at the cursor.

NPC services come from the NPC's title (Innkeeper, Flight Master, Weapon Merchant). A title the addon doesn't know is shown as it is.

Shift and combat are both events the game sends the moment they happen, so the switch is instant.

Out of reach: how far away a creature is, and what it drops. No style shows either.

All units are examples. Timberling Ravager is a level 24 elite wolf for the quest "Ravaged Pelts"; your party is Jokke (rogue), Thalianne (warrior), Mira (priest) and Brokk (hunter).
